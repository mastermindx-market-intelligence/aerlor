# AERLOR | Verified Buyer Authority and Channel Shortlist
**Evidence refresh: 2026-11-04 | Public-safe market development.** This is a list of **prospective channels and research targets, not customers, qualified sales opportunities or a contact campaign**. Source links point to public company pages. No outreach was performed.

## Executive change

AERLOR's core obstacle is **not simply average hotel spending capacity**: it is purchase authority, substitution restrictions, proof of safe product performance, and acquisition effort for each finite equipment order. Our strongest initial targets are organizations with *documented purchasing or guestroom equipment-specification responsibilities*, not necessarily the world's largest hotel brands.

### How to read the ranks

- **P0 – verify an actual funded buying mechanism:** buyer-role or program is documented and relevant to room appliances. Does not mean willingness to buy AERLOR.
- **P1 – attractive segment, authority unconfirmed:** a portfolio or partner could be useful; first task is routing to an actual category owner.
- **P2 – potential later scale path:** channel is real but onboarding, brand standards or incumbents may create significant friction.

Do not compute an invented purchase-conversion probability from these rankings. A true qualified opportunity requires a named purchasing entity, product category allowed, budget/project, decision date, commercial price acceptance and technical readiness.

## Procurement findings to actively test

1. **Central purchasing can be real** (Crestline, Noble House, Concord, InnVest), but ability to **mandate an upgrade** is a separate question.
2. **GPO economics differ** from a purchase order. DayBlink says suppliers fund its business and hotel participants select their categories. Price every fee and partner activation obligation before forecasting volume.
3. **Procurement agents are not resellers.** Benjamin West explicitly states it acts as an owner's fiduciary purchasing agent; do not pitch undisclosed distributor margins or referral commissions.
4. **Large hotel portfolios may be worse than small owners** when a franchise standard blocks substitution. Use exact flags and product categories, not company-wide generalizations.
5. **Existing premium brands have a channel.** SLX's T3 and Conair listings disprove the notion that premium hairdryers are absent from hospitality; determine which net price and performance conditions would create a superior offer.
6. **The scale ceiling is driven by funded room events.** New openings and renovations remove the hardest part of the original sale—persuading a hotel to retire perfectly functioning items.
