# AERLOR | Proposed Operating and Distribution Model
**2026-11-04 | Public-safe | Every partner and supplier arrangement is prospective, not secured**

## 1. Narrow scope and long-term ambition

**Initial business:** one carefully qualified high-speed guestroom dryer with documented commercial-use suitability, optional finish variants, and reliable local replacements. Deliver to independent upscale/luxury hotels and funded openings/renovations, directly or via appropriately structured partners.

**Expansion options:** lighted makeup mirrors and shared styling tools as partner-supplied services; later DTC retail and selected adjacent appliances *only* if the channel and product economics justify them. Do not launch as an unrestricted toiletries/disposable importer.

## 2. Where Aerlor wins

Against legacy basic dryers: room-ready performance and a more coherent premium presentation.

Against cheap high-speed consumer dryers: actual commercial-use support, independently verified suitability, predictable stock, warranty administration and hotel purchasing ease.

Against well-known luxury brands: a potentially more affordable whole-property proposition **without pretending to offer the same brand recognition**.

Against incumbent hospitality distributors: specialist category knowledge, an integrated room/housekeeping process, a compelling buyer-led spec and competitively quoted total acquisition cost.

**AERLOR does not have these advantages yet**. Each is a target to validate.

## 3. Channel roles must not be conflated

| Route | Buyer / agreement | Revenue recognition concept | Key risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct property | Hotel owner/operator or approved purchasing entity buys AERLOR | We invoice delivered product and support as contracted | High customer-acquisition effort |
| Multi-property owner/operator | Central agreement plus property-level orders/rollout budgets | Actual purchase orders, not total owned rooms | Each property may still need approval |
| GPO / collection | Preferred supplier/channel program; hotel remains purchaser | Fees/rebates only as agreed; **no implied orders** | Passive catalog entry |
| Project OS&E specifier | Include product as qualified alternate for funded opening/renovation | Client or appointed distributor places order | Lead times; brand standards |
| Stocking distributor | Distributor buys inventory at net sell-in | Our revenue is sell-in, not end-hotel selling price | Large wholesale discount, returns, credit, stock pressure |
| Amenities strategic partner | Distribution, white-label, licensed collection, or development/supply agreement | Negotiated supply economics | IP/branding, exclusivity, partner dependence |
| Consumer retail | Direct or approved retail purchase | Real retail revenue net fulfillment/CAC/returns | Expensive brand-building, channel conflict |

**Preferred launch sequence:** direct/reference paid pilots -> proven operator groups and project specifiers -> carefully negotiated distributor/GPO program -> retail/adjacent categories.

## 4. Product and SKU architecture

- **One electrical platform** first. Avoid different internal motors/control boards for visually differentiated tiers unless there is a tested customer benefit and product/safety approval path.
- Initial variant(s) should be chosen through a real buyer preference test, with honest statements of materials and capabilities.
- Treat styling tools as a **prebookable lending program** with a small measured inventory: no promise of universal availability or mandatory permanent provision in every room.
- Mirrors should first be qualified from an existing hotel-ready supplier before any AERLOR-branded development.
- Consumables (dental kits/slippers) can be fulfilled through authorized amenity partners; they introduce their own quality, packaging and reorder complexity.

## 5. North American first-market operating choice

Two reasonable starting routes:
- **Canada validation cluster:** geographically manageable from existing founder operating base and smaller pilot logistics; requires Canadian-specific electrical and importer review.
- **US scale market:** much larger hotel base and network opportunity; exact US electrical product, customs, warranty and sales-tax compliance required.

Decision should be made after obtaining viable exact-model approval and target-buyer feedback. Never assume a single product claim/certification or tariff treatment covers both countries.

## 6. Quality and logistics (proposed)

**Supplier control:** one approved legal factory, signed specification/BOM, no substitutions without requalification, lot/serial tracking, independent pre-shipment QA, batch samples, recall cooperation and clear tooling/IP title.

**Importer/distribution:** verified correct origin/classification, complete duty estimates, one primary regional stockholding arrangement, damage inspection and serial-traceable RMA records.

**Hotel installation:** confirm property standard and electrical fit; ship cases/spares; property replaces/installs per agreed schedule; retire old items via property-approved reuse/recycling partner if contracted. No promise that all older working units will be discarded.

**Service:** written guestroom-use warranty, defined swap eligibility, replacement-inventory policy, return/recycling procedure, defect root-cause loop and incident escalation. Do not make fast-shipping guarantees before fulfillment contracts exist.

**Data handling:** only capture permitted account contact information through an actual compliant endpoint. Site preview currently does not transmit leads; see [LAUNCH.md](../../LAUNCH.md).

## 7. Financial discipline

Maintain a **restricted** deal/unit model with:
- Purchase quantity and genuine installation/rollout event.
- Two-sided distinction between hotel sellout and manufacturer sell-in.
- Ex-works, packaging, QC, international freight, duties/tariffs, broker costs, warehousing, final-mile freight, damages, warranty reserve, channel fees, commission, customer success and sales expenses.
- Product liability insurance, sample/test/legal/tooling costs as separate fixed and stage-gate items.
- Cash conversion: inventory, deposits, A/R, supplier terms and financing.
- Scenarios: standalone pilot, 10 properties, 100 properties, multi-year installed-base replacements.
- Worst-case sensitivity: fully loaded landed-cost overrun, low reseller price, poor yield, slow conversion, high return/failure rate, 60–90-day receivables.

**No purchase order without a coherent source-of-funds/working-capital plan.**

## 8. What a funded channel partnership must deliver

Minimum viable agreement terms:
1. Named category owner and participating pilot/properties or target projects.
2. Transparent distributor vs GPO vs agency compensation; direct-channel boundaries.
3. Launch plan with scheduled sales enablement, samples, introductions, category review or opening order.
4. Return rights, unsold-stock obligations, payment and rebate timing.
5. Warranty work allocation, replacements, insurance and recall handling.
6. Data/reporting and reasonable account registration.
7. Bounded exclusivity only against real minimum order or activation commitments.
8. Termination rights, remaining inventory treatment and release of exclusivity if inactive.

An agreement promising access to hundreds of hotels **without an activation and purchase path** is not an order and should not be valued as one.

## 9. Brand and website alignment

The existing site is a concept with a truthful no-sales/no-lead-capture boundary. It can assist buyer research as a **preview** but must not claim:
- Shipping inventory, actual warranties or certificates.
- Established MSRP reductions or real retail transactions.
- Hotel endorsements, pilots, contracts or performance that have not occurred.

Next website work follows verified product selection and authorized commercial identity. Protect existing visual work and tests; submit changes as reviewable PRs.

## 10. Execution dependencies

Commercially significant prospect outreach should follow an approved representation/identity. Formal buyer samples follow exact product readiness and user-authorized commercial terms. Hotel installation follows product/legal/operational approvals. Broader stock follows purchase orders, contribution and cash gates. Consumer launch follows supportability and truth-in-pricing review.
