# AERLOR | Corporate Identity, Importer and Trademark Setup Decision
**Research note dated 2026-11-04 | Public-safe | No entity created, domain purchased, trademark filed or legal advice supplied**

## Purpose

AERLOR's concept website and GitHub repository are real. **A business entity, bank/KYC setup, importer of record, approved commercial communication identity, insurance and trademark rights are not evidenced.** These are distinct requirements for sending truthful commercial proposals, buying samples and ultimately invoicing hotels. A North American-sounding name is not proof of company domicile or product origin.

**Board decision needed:** Which entity actually owns the brand/designs, holds supplier contracts, imports products, invoices hotel customers, accepts product liability and owns the intellectual property?

A founder's residence and tax status, intended shareholder group, jurisdiction of management and existing companies matter. **Do not choose a US C corporation, LLC or Canadian company from branding arguments alone.**

## Candidate legal structures to analyze with qualified cross-border counsel/CPA

| Structure | When it might fit | What counsel/accountant must resolve | Not a shortcut for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **US entity (e.g. Delaware C corporation)** | Main customers, stock and payment collection expected in United States; potential US brand headquarters and institutional financing route. | Ownership, shareholder residence and taxes, federal/state filings, foreign ownership reporting, where management/operations actually occur, sales-tax nexus, banking/insurance and importer details. Delaware requires registered agent. | Calling Chinese-made dryers 'Made in USA'; bypassing imports, safety tests, trademark clearance or tax filing. |
| **Canadian operating company selling into US** | Canadian pilots, management and early operations plus exports to US hotels; lower initial entity complexity depending on existing capabilities. | US nonresident importer of record, customs bond and broker, US sales/local tax and economic nexus, US income tax and treaty exposure, Canadian GST/HST and corporate tax, insurance and warranties. | Pretending to be US-domiciled; exemption from customs duty merely by invoicing from Canada. |
| **Canadian parent + US operating subsidiary** | Multi-country sourcing, distribution and service after product/market demand is established. | Intercompany transfer pricing, entity financing and trademark ownership, foreign affiliate reporting, related party transactions, US/Canadian corporate tax and permanent establishment issues. | Reducing total reporting automatically; complexity and compliance costs can increase materially. |
| **Existing founder-controlled entity** | Might expedite a legitimate contracting/communication identity in discovery. | Whether use is authorized/wise, ownership of brand/IP, liability separation, accounting/tax and insurance, transaction terms. | Mixing unrelated business liabilities or assuming an existing company's market approvals apply. |

**US foreign-owner rule:** Delaware code permits any person to incorporate regardless of residence/domicile, but entities need a Delaware registered agent. [Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc01/) / [Division of Corporations](https://corp.delaware.gov/howtoform/).

**US reporting caveat:** IRS [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) cover specified reportable related-party transactions for 25%-foreign-owned US corporations and foreign-owned US disregarded entities, including potentially formation contributions/distributions, and indicate substantial nonfiling penalties. Whether it applies depends on exact ownership and transactions. Do not assume a simple US LLC eliminates filings.

**Canadian cross-border caveat:** CRA [T1134 information](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/foreign-reporting/information-returns-relating-foreign-affiliates.html) discusses reporting of relevant foreign affiliates and their financial statements. Who holds the US entity and Canadian taxpayer filing requirements require case-by-case analysis.

**US importer number:** [CBP](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/article/Article-1066?language=en_US) describes an EIN or customs-assigned importer number through CBP Form 5106 for eligible entities without an IRS tax number. Actual importer eligibility and bond/entry mechanics need a customs broker.

## Minimum contractor/transaction chain — before accepting payment

1. Choose the legitimate manufacturer under a contracted commercial supply and warranty framework.
2. Decide the entity taking **title and import risk**, verify customs number, broker, bond, landed duty and importer compliance.
3. Decide the **actual seller of record** to hotels and the states/provinces/countries where sales or VAT/GST, payroll and local taxes may apply.
4. Put insurance in place covering product liability, completed operations and territory; verify hotel vendor requirements.
5. Establish business bank accounts/payment acceptance and applicable customer invoicing/returns process.
6. Preserve brand/IP assignment to the appropriate company; verify package labels, country-of-origin and truthful US presence claims.
7. Use written employment/agency/distributor scopes with disclosed compensation and no unauthorized legal commitments.

## Trademark and domain sequence

- Commission a real clearance search across US/Canada and possible later EU/UK markets, including **confusingly similar** marks and common-law use. Searching 'AERLOR' alone on the web is not sufficient.
- **USPTO requirement:** foreign-domiciled trademark applicants and registrants must use a US-licensed attorney in US trademark proceedings. [USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/do-i-need-attorney).
- Establish ownership of name, logo and any industrial design in proper legal entity, not an unrelated personal inbox by accident.
- Register the target domain only via a Chairman-approved account/payment after preliminary clearance and ensure ownership/renewal access controls; availability is not reservation.
- Do not claim 'American-made', 'American company' or 'US-designed' unless each is legally and factually supportable, and avoid using an inflated MSRP to create fictitious luxury positioning. See [USPTO clearance guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks).

## Recommended board-level decision sequence (no filings executed)

1. **Define actual control:** intended shareholder, jurisdiction of true operations, target importer, target first market, location of inventory and customers.
