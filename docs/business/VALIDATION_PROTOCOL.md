# AERLOR | Commercial Validation Protocol
**Working spec — 2026-11-04 | Not evidence of existing product or commitments**

This is an executable **customer-discovery-to-paid-pilot** protocol. The site currently uses PRO/LITE concept language; the real commercial SKU naming remains a brand decision. Do not represent the design concept as a certified production model.

## 1. Define the buying unit correctly

For each hotel account record:
- Property, property type/flag, room count and expected segment (standard rooms, suites, all rooms).
- Actual property **owner**, **management company/operator**, relevant **brand standards**, purchasing organization/distributor, the legal purchasing entity, and payer.
- Owner/GM/rooms/housekeeping/engineering/procurement roles: sponsor, approver, veto holder.
- Current exact dryer model, age if known, delivered price, annual failures/replacements, complaints and current spare stock.
- Renovation/new opening/refresh window; specific budget source and planned buying date.
- Product category requirements; compliance and insurance needs; exact net offer/contract terms; proof of funded scope.
- Next action, responsible person, evidence link and disposition.

**Qualification rule:** a list of names is not pipeline. Qualified means a named approver, use case, economic scope, timing, and a pathway to a funded decision.

Do not store personal business contact information or negotiated offers in this public repository.

## 2. Interview order (avoid selling before finding budget)

### Cohort A — purchasing networks / owner groups
Goal: identify who can approve multiple hotels, who must approve alternates, and how the purchasing network activates a vendor.

Suggested prompts:
1. Can you point to an actual 2026–27 opening, renovation or guestroom-experience initiative where dryers could be considered?
2. What do hotels pay today **net delivered**? Which products/brands have passed their standard?
3. What is the actual approval chain (owner, brand, operator, purchasing, engineering)?
4. Does a supplier agreement produce introductions, catalog listing, active promotions, commitments, or nothing beyond eligibility?
5. Who bears inventory, freight, warranty, returns and credit/receivable risk?
6. What is required for a paid pilot with a decision date?
7. Would you buy an **unknown** brand? What would differentiate it from Laifen, Conair and high-end branded alternatives?
8. Is a styling-tool lending service useful, or already offered?

### Cohort B — property operators
Goal: determine whether a financed upgrade decision is in reach. Interview GMs, director of rooms, executive housekeeping and engineering together when possible.

### Cohort C — design/specification and category distributors
Goal: target funded OS&E projects and validate realistic channel margin, onboarding cost, price protection, lead times, returns and minimum orders.

## 3. Three distinct tests; no inference substitution

| Test | Protocol | Must conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technical / operations** | Compare AERLOR candidate vs legacy incumbent and one credible low-cost high-speed dryer, same relevant hair types/conditions, independent measurements and staff care trials. | Evidence for performance, cleaning burden, safety and reliability. |
| **Brand and price** | Randomize anonymized performance demonstration, then show true branding/prices; measure selected model and rationale among qualified hotel purchasers. | Whether luxury-positioned unknown branding changes *purchase willingness* at a real hotel price. |
| **Commercial** | Have paid pilots, named approver and pre-agreed post-pilot purchase review. | Whether a hotel will pay and then order a rollout, not merely compliment the sample. |

Include diverse hair routines, styling preferences, hair textures and lengths. No claims of reducing hair damage unless supported by appropriate testing.

## 4. Supplier RFQ / qualification

Obtain a comparable commercial response for **at least three independently designed candidate platforms** with one exact model and voltage variant each.

Collect:
- Exact manufacturer, exporter and legal entity; factory and production site.
- Existing model photos and dimensions vs concept renderings.
- BOM, motor/control/thermal components, cord/plug, ingress/immersion protection, housing materials, certifications/report IDs, test lab, serials and batch-traceability.
- MOQ, quotation date, currency, Incoterms, ex-works price by volume, packaging, accessories, replacement filters, master case specs, spare-part availability.
- Production QA controls (incoming inspection, burn-in, failure thresholds, change approval, traceability, CAPA, recall cooperation).
- Approval for US/Canada guestroom commercial use from competent lab/counsel as applicable; full return/repair/replacement policy.
- Supplier insurance and contractual performance guarantees, realistic deposits, delivered shipping lead time and component availability.
- Patent/design FTO evidence **specific to the proposed housing and mechanism**, not a general assertion that patents have expired.
- Customs broker’s all-in duty calculation for the exact origin, commodity, entry date and import scenario.

**Reject** unsupported factory safety logos, copied ornamental designs, unsubstantiated RPM-only marketing and assumptions that plastic/metal changes inherit approval.

## 5. Product acceptance scorecard

A scored test may cover:
- Drying time under consistent and appropriate controls (never infer every hair texture).
- Surface/exhaust temperature and thermal cycling, including abnormal filter obstruction.
- Noise under a consistent measurement method and distance.
- Weight, balance and handling, including cord flexibility and grip.
- Appropriate diffuser/concentrator options; magnetic attachment security.
- Filter access and cleaning time; how staff recognize a blocked filter.
- Controls usable without training, safe storage after use and real outlets.
- Drop, shipping, lint, repeated operation, voltage and plug tests with lab-approved protocols.
- Appearance after repeated cleaning; housing material tradeoffs.
- Replacement and field-traceability process.

Publish marketing claims only after exact-model testing supports them.

## 6. Paid pilot design

**Proposed**: three unrelated properties, approximately 20–30 installed rooms/property, six to eight weeks of observation **after** legal/safety readiness. Adjust based on buyer needs.

Before installation:
- Signed pilot purchase or paid evaluation terms, approved model and insurance.
- Named budget owner, operations/engineering reviewer, current dryer baseline, agreed cleaning protocol.
- Pre-set guest feedback method and consistent sampling; privacy protection for guests/staff.
- Written post-pilot review date, target rollout scope, delivered price, lead time and acceptance rules.
- Track installation by room **without posting room/guest identifiers publicly**.

During:
- Room-level use uptime, defects, cleaning time, guest preference, requested styling tools, loss/theft/returns, replacement lead time.
- Resolve safety incidents immediately under established product incident/recall response.
- Do not infer revenue/ADR uplift from informal anecdotes.

After:
- Obtain a funded order/PO, a priced conditional rollout schedule, a documented rejection reason, or a clear **no decision**. Each has different evidence value.
- Reconcile product contribution and cash, not only gross hotel revenue.

## 7. Sequential scale gates

**Go / advance to next stage when:**
- An owner/buyer acknowledges a specific upgrade or funded project, not reactive-only spares.
- Exact proposed product is viable for intended market and pilot use, with IP and compliance checks completed.
- A paid evaluation produces positive enough guest *and* staff outcomes.
- More than one independent buyer approves a material rollout and unit economics survive channel fees/warranty/cash.
- Partner route has named activation and sales commitments, not only a listing.

**Pause/redesign when:**
- Existing brand standards prevent a near-term alternative.
- Product fails safety or key hair-routine tests.
- Supplier price is viable only using ambiguous taxes/duties or unsecured warranty assumptions.
- Channel wants inventory/exclusivity without performance commitments.
- Pilots don't convert, and the most plausible explanation is that properties simply don't value the upgrade enough.
