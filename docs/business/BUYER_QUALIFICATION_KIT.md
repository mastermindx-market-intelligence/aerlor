# AERLOR | Buyer Qualification Kit — Procurement, Operators and Projects
**Working sales-development document, 2026-11-04. All language below is PRELAUNCH; no outbound messages have been sent.**

## Commercial objective

Find a **repeatable buying route** for a premium-appearing, appropriately approved guestroom hairdryer at a hotel-relevant total cost. AERLOR is currently a prelaunch concept and has **no established production model, approval, case study, inventory, partnership or published commercial price**. Do not represent the existing design preview as a production product.

**Avoid the low-leverage motion:** a GM compliments a sample, requests one and never raises a funded order. Instead pursue parties that control specific budgets, project specs or several properties.

## Three buyer pathways: choose before writing

| Path | Who authorizes buying | What triggers a purchase | Who can block it | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Project / opening / renovation** | Owner's project budget through OS&E purchasing/agent | A scheduled procurement scope requiring guestroom equipment | Brand standard, engineer, procurement specification, legal/insurance | Actual project, scope, spec freeze date, purchasing agent, eligible source, tender timing |
| **Owner/operator portfolio standard** | Owner investment and/or delegated operating budget | Owner wants a differentiated amenity or consistent quality across hotels | Flag requirements, property-level owner or management agreement, purchasing program | Named hotel subset, authorized decision-maker, current unit, expected cost, money source |
| **Distributor / GPO / amenities partner** | Stocking buyer and/or hotel member making orders | Existing customer demand and acceptable reseller contribution | Vendor onboarding, stocking, category fit, returns, contracts | Net sell-in economics and an **activation + purchase plan**, not directory listing |

### The first question that prevents wasted sales effort

> “Who has authority to decide the exact model of guestroom electrical appliances at the participating property, and are alternates allowed under the hotel flag and any current sourcing agreement?”

If the source is mandated, investigate the brand-approval route **before** putting effort into an individual property.

## Structured 20-minute discovery call

Use an evidence-first conversation, with no product performance assertions.

**Opening (30 seconds):**
“I'm researching whether upscale hotels would consider a more thoughtfully designed, hotel-supported high-speed dryer and optional getting-ready tools. We are **prelaunch** and do not yet have an approved production unit to sell. I want to understand your procurement and operating requirements so we don't build something your team could never approve.”

**Decision route (4 minutes):**
- “What are the current guestroom hairdryer models, and are they standardized by brand, management company, owner, project designer or local purchasing?”
- “Who pays for guestroom appliance upgrades if units are still functioning? Operating supplies, FF&E reserve, owner capex, or project OS&E?”
- “Is a compliant alternate source possible? Which team approves the electrical product and brand fit?”
- “Who ultimately signs the purchase order? Can one decision cover multiple properties?”

**Replacement vs funded upgrade (4 minutes):**
- “Do you usually replace failed units from a spare pool, install upgrades in selected room categories, or refresh a whole property?”
- “Have you considered an upgrade in the last 24 months? What stopped it?”
- “What hotel-use warranty, spare stock, theft/loss, housekeeping, cable, attachments and filter concerns affect the choice?”
- “At your most recent purchase, what was the **net delivered cost** and who supplied it?” (Accept if confidential; don't pressure.)

**Commercial and project timing (4 minutes):**
- “Are any openings, conversions, renovations or funded amenity projects still before guestroom OS&E specification freeze?”
- “What quantity, case pack, delivery window and payment terms would actually be appropriate?”
- “What documents must a new electrical supplier provide before you can evaluate it?”

**Brand and product objection test (5 minutes):**
- “What would make a new, unfamiliar North American-positioned brand acceptable compared with Dyson, Laifen, Conair, JVD or an existing distributor model?”
- “Do guests recognize particular brand names, or do measured performance, presentation and support matter more?”
- “Do you want permanent room placement, premium-suite placement, or lending inventory for stylers/mirrors?”
- “Would you evaluate the options *at realistic net prices* and specify the conditions for a paid evaluation?”

**Exit (2 minutes):**
- Confirm approver, specific next internal action, product requirements, anticipated decision date, and whether a **paid product evaluation** is plausible after independent product readiness.
- Record explicit negative decisions and reasons. “Interested” with no buyer or project is **not qualified**.

## Follow-up and decision rule

One polite follow-up may clarify routing. Avoid multi-inbox scraping or repeated unsolicited commercial messages. Apply market-specific B2B marketing and privacy requirements before email; in Canada assess CASL's restrictions and applicable consent exceptions; in the US assess CAN-SPAM and applicable state rules. Do not use a public site's generic mailbox as permission for unrestricted campaigns.

For **every positive interview**, require:
- Real property/project and actual buying organization;
- Owner/brand/engineering approval chain;
- Actual timing, scope and source of budget;
- Exact model and technical documentation requirements;
- Buyer-level willingness to pay for a qualified **paid** sample/pilot;
- Calendar date and owner of next step.

For **negative interviews**, classify:
1. price/cost; 2. insufficient guest benefit; 3. brand recognition; 4. incumbent contracted; 5. mandatory source/flag; 6. technical risk; 7. replacement-only policy; 8. bad project timing; 9. unknown.

Never claim an interview, signed intent or approved product exists until recorded evidence supports it.

## Suggested discovery goal (not booked appointments)

- 5 owners/operators with a visible centralized procurement function;
- 4 project OS&E procurement teams;
- 3 hotel procurement networks/amenities partners;
- 3 independent luxury properties to test operational detail;
- 3 distributors on price, margin and warranty handling.

Aim for **18** high-quality conversations if response access is viable; the number matters less than actual buying authorization and funded opportunities. Store private account notes in a restricted CRM.
