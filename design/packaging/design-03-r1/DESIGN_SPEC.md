# Contour R1 / Product and guestroom design specification

**PROPOSAL · 2026-11-04 · Industrial-design intent, not CAD or an approved electrical product**

## 1. Outcome and design rationale

A guest should find the dryer, identify the controls, choose a suitable routine and return it without confusion. Housekeeping should be able to see what is missing, inspect the cord and filter, and distinguish a ready unit from one requiring service. Procurement should receive traceable units with simple replenishment packaging. These are **design objectives**, not observed AERLOR results.

The existing Paper/site studies and strategy PR #1 provide brand context. They do not freeze product architecture. This proposal keeps the warm paper, dark ink and restrained editorial character of the current design, while exploring a different product silhouette: a shallow rounded-rectangle outlet, a tapered head, a continuous curved shoulder and an oblong grip with a fine longitudinal facet. The image is independently generated from the saved brief, without competitor reference images. That process does **not** establish patent, design, trademark or trade-dress clearance.

The differentiation hypothesis is useful guest and staff experience: understandable controls, maintainable intake, organized accessories and traceable bulk replenishment. Neither motor speed, a premium finish nor the appearance of a North American brand proves a purchase advantage. The product benchmark in PR #1 already documents relevant incumbents; current primary sources in [the ledger](SOURCE_LEDGER.md) inform the specific requirements below.

## 2. Proposed architecture and unresolved interfaces

| Element | Design intent | Engineering input needed before freeze |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head and shoulder | Softly chamfered asymmetric wedge; rounded-rectangle discharge; continuous shoulder into grip | Air-path and thermal feasibility; motor/impeller location; full internal envelope; acoustic and molding analysis |
| Grip | Oblong section, slight taper and discernible side facet; avoid sharp seams at contact points | Small/large-hand mockups, left/right-handed use, mass and center of gravity, thermal map, cord force and slip assessment |
| Controls | Distinct physical power/air selector with an unambiguous OFF position; separate heat selection and cool function | Circuit behavior and safe default approved by engineer; force/travel, tactile separation, labels and accidental-actuation tests |
| Intake/filter | Visible rear service area, accessible from a dry inspection posture | Actual filter mechanism, obstruction and hair-ingress assessment, required clearances, safe cleaning method and access boundaries |
| Accessory interface | Positive, intelligible retention; concentrating and diffusing routines both evaluated | Mechanical interface, hot-touch handling, attachment mass, retention and breakage testing; no assumed magnets |
| Cord/plug | Flexible routing from strain relief; protection plug kept visible and supported in storage | Exact market cordset/protection device, plug dimensions, accessible test/reset operation if fitted, bend limits and approved instructions |
| Identification | Legible model/market/serial and approved safety information on durable surfaces | Exact text, languages, material compatibility, certifier and legal approval; no marks are supplied by this concept |

The rear-filter position, form and controls may conflict with a candidate platform. SOURCE-02 must return the actual envelope and supported changes; DESIGN-03 then adapts the concept or rejects the fit. Do not require new tooling merely to preserve the rendering. Do not change a certified enclosure, finish, cord, control board or air path on an assumption that existing approval transfers.

### Control sequence for evaluation

The proposed interaction sequence is **OFF → select air → select heat as needed → use cool function as needed → OFF → unplug under approved instructions → allow complete cooling before storage**. An engineer must decide whether selected heat persists, resets or is constrained on restart. This document does not prescribe circuit design or a safety-rated reset behavior.

Use words and tactile differentiation in addition to visual icons. Distinguish heat selection from cool function and airflow; do not make color or tiny illuminated indicators the only state cue. Exact switch geometry, language set, contrast and print durability require review. The appearance study omits final legends, so it cannot be used as an operating guide. Its presentation pose also does not demonstrate countertop stability or an approved standing position.

Digital concept references: Pearl `#E7E0D3`, Stone `#A9A092`, Graphite `#393A37`, Recess `#5C5A53`, ink `#2C2A25`. They are screen swatches, **not** approved Pantone/RAL values, print separations or resin specifications. Avoid soft-touch coatings or exposed decorative metal as defaults. Both introduce compatibility, wear or thermal questions. A finish that cannot pass required tests is removed, even if preferred in an appearance study. No recyclability, antimicrobial, hair-protection or sustainability claim follows from the palette.

## 4. Guest experience and inclusive evaluation

Different hair lengths, textures and routines change the value of airflow, heat controls and attachments. Dyson's product-specific attachment guidance is evidence that attachments address distinct routines, not proof of AERLOR results [SRC-02]. Include straight, wavy, curly and coily routines, different lengths/densities, and participants who choose gentle airflow or diffusion. Do not use gender as a proxy for routine. Record handedness, grip comfort and accessibility needs with consent in the restricted research workspace.

### Proposed formative protocol; not executed

First use nonpowered appearance/ergonomic mockups. Any powered evaluation waits for the product-safety owner to authorize a suitable exact sample and test setting. MARKET-01 and the integrator choose participants and privacy handling; no recruitment or guest contact is authorized here.

1. **Find and understand:** place an unbranded concept or approved sample in the proposed room storage position. Ask the participant to locate it, identify OFF, heat, air and the cool function, and explain where it should be stored after use. Record errors and task time without coaching. Use one-handed, reduced-dexterity operation as a usability benchmark [SRC-09]; no claim of ADA compliance for this portable concept is made.
2. **Grip and reach:** observe both hands, comfortable wrist position, switch reach, accidental changes and cord drag; include seated use where relevant. Weigh the sample with and without cord/attachment under a stated method. Do not compare unknown mass from rendering.
3. **Routine coverage:** under an approved protocol, compare the appropriate diffuser/concentrator or no attachment for the participant's actual routine. Record comfort and usability separately from measured drying performance. No single participant establishes population performance.
4. **Care discoverability:** ask staff to identify the filter, access permitted cleaning points, find model/serial and identify an incomplete accessory set. Observe before training, then repeat using the approved procedure.
5. **Recovery:** demonstrate how a unit marked damaged/unknown would be taken out of service. Do not ask guests or staff to simulate faults, obstruct airflow or perform electrical tests.

**Proposed formative acceptance:** every observed critical confusion (failure to find OFF, intended energized storage, unsafe wrapping or mistaken ready/quarantine state) triggers redesign and re-evaluation before progression. Report the actual denominator and tasks. Noncritical success targets and comparison margins must be agreed before the test, with the intended user sample and buyer; do not claim statistical validation from a small design round.

## 5. Room integration

The default exploration is portable use with a removable dry-storage tray. Mounted, hardwired, tethered or retractable-cord options remain separate alternatives requiring hotel engineering, procurement and exact-product review. No wall fixing, electrical work or installed hotel relationship is implied.

### Measurement sheet required from MARKET-01 and hotel engineering

| Measure/observation | Record | Design consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawer/cabinet | Clear internal width/depth/height, opening envelope, slides/hinges, usable lift-out space | Tray and finger-clearance envelope; verify closure without cord/plug trapping |
| Counter | Dry available footprint, surface finish, edge conditions, access to mirror | Tray location and stability; no placement at sink/bath edge or moisture-prone position assumed safe |
| Outlet | Location/orientation, adjacent obstructions, exact protection-plug fit and access | Check real plug rather than generic slim-plug silhouette; no adapter/extension assumption |
| Reach/routing | Standing/seated location, cord path, door/drawer pinch points and walking path | Cord length/flexibility requirement and snag risk; full actual length TBD |
| Staff workflow | Current cleaning materials and instructions, inspection cadence, spare storage | Compatibility testing, service-access and replacement handoff |
| Accessories | Current requests, missing items and approved provision method | Universal room provision vs accessible request pool is a buyer decision |
| Security | Actual loss pattern and permitted inventory controls | Prefer serial/inventory process first; no punitive deposit, tracking device or tether assumed |

Measurements must be taken on the actual room type; a single hero-suite picture is insufficient. The same product may require different storage inserts by room configuration. If the protection plug cannot fit safely, or the drawer can close on the cord, the room configuration fails; don't solve it with an unapproved adapter or altered cordset.

## 6. Housekeeping and safe state separation

The room tray and optional bag are proposed **cold, dry storage only**, not heat-resistant equipment or a powered holder. Do not depict or instruct storage of an energized, hot or damp dryer. No fixed cooling time or touch-temperature threshold is invented. The exact manufacturer instructions and competent engineering review must establish cooling, inspection and safe handling [SRC-01, SRC-03, SRC-05]. A “cool shot” does not prove the housing or attachments are cool enough to store.

Proposed staff flow, subject to approved procedures:

- Remove returned items from the clean/ready pool; keep uninspected returns in a separate identified area.
- Ensure the unit is unplugged and fully cooled according to approved instructions before inspection or storage. Use the actual cordset instructions for any checks.
- Inspect cord/plug/strain relief, casing, grille, permitted filter surfaces, labels and accessories. A damaged, wet, abnormal or uncertain unit is tagged **OUT OF SERVICE** and routed to the responsible engineering owner; staff do not repair it or energize it to investigate.
- Clean only by the exact approved manufacturer method using approved hotel agents. Do not immerse the appliance, spray openings, infer chemical resistance or advertise sanitization from a wipe.
- Return a complete, dry, accepted unit to its visible storage positions; put the actual cord in a loose separate channel respecting the supplier's bend limits, without wrapping tightly around the housing.

The source manufacturer may prescribe different cleaning methods for different filters; no competitor cleaning frequency or wash instruction is transferred to AERLOR. Record maintenance events with internal asset identifiers; guest/room/person records remain restricted. A QR code is optional and must lead to approved instructions without unapproved guest data collection; printed essential instructions remain available offline.

### Later styling-loaner option

The future case has a removable inventory insert, visibly distinct clean/ready and returned/uninspected status, and a dry return/quarantine workflow. Its proposed cavities do **not** authorize any curler, straightener, mirror or steamer SKU. Hot styling tools require their own qualified cooldown and handling/storage system; the dryer tray is not automatically suitable. MARKET-01 must show buyer demand and staffing capacity before adjacent-product development proceeds under #12.

## 7. Engineering, safety and IP gates

CPSC identifies hand-supported dryers without required integral immersion protection as substantial product hazards; its FAQ also states that this 15(j) rule does not itself create CPSA section 14(a) testing/certification obligations [SRC-01]. This is not a blanket exemption from other applicable requirements or procurement conditions. SOURCE-02 and qualified reviewers own exact-market scope, current standards, lab evidence and approval. Canadian approval must be evaluated separately [SRC-04].

Before a physical guestroom pilot, the company still needs the actual 120 V/60 Hz US candidate where applicable, separate Canadian scope if intended, verified safety/thermal/cordset evidence, authentic labeling, hotel engineering acceptance, appropriate insurance, hotel-use warranty, rights review and authorized pilot terms. Images, a generic block plug, a claim by a factory or an AERLOR review checklist cannot substitute for that evidence.

Before a dimensioned enclosure release, obtain: datum scheme; product and accessory drawings; internal keep-out volumes; resin/coating grade; wall thickness; draft and shrinkage requirements; joint/seam and fastener strategy; assembly/service access; tolerances and stackup; filter/airflow paths; attachment retention; thermal clearances; cord/plug strain relief; durable label zones; design-for-assembly review; and explicit change-control responsibility. Every numerical value is TBD pending measured samples and qualified engineering. Tooling and fabrication are not authorized by this packet.

## 8. Review decision

Accept this R1 only as an evidence-linked design proposal ready for cross-lane review. A good rendering does not pass procurement, user, manufacturability, IP or product-safety gates. The next revision should replace unknowns with measured data and recorded decisions, retaining a visible distinction between proposed, observed, tested and approved states.
